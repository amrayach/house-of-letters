9-3-92

Another evening. The weather was warm today, relatively warm. And yet I feel cold.

A cold coming from inside me.

Today it became clear to us from the newspapers and the radio that everything is postponed now, until 12 March and after.

Because the president will give a speech that day.

He will speak about important matters inside and outside the country, so we are waiting for Thursday to hear what he says.

Today I wanted to comment on what I wrote yesterday. I felt that maybe I had moved away from the truth.

But my mood is not right.

A little while ago my thoughts drifted toward leaving prison, toward home, toward Randa, the children, and the family. When I caught my thoughts doing that, I scolded them for going too far and tried to bring them back to the rules.

What I am trying to write is not poetry. I know very well that I am not good at that. It is only an idea. Today while I was walking, this came to my mind:

Fear is a sea, and I am a fish in it. Freedom is a star, and I reach out to catch it. Hope is a butterfly that always escapes from my hand.

10-3-92

The nights have become long for me, and maybe the days too. That is the reality of these days. I have been living my hidden nervous tension through the weakness of my digestive system.

Besides work, there is of course reading and trying to catch what is new, and listening to the radio for the same reason. Sometimes the goal is not knowledge itself, but something else—maybe argument, maybe discussion. Maybe the hidden goal behind all this is proving one’s existence to the people here first. Some people are satisfied with proving their existence here so they can feel balanced. Reading and gathering information for discussion may be one of these methods. Excelling in something else may be another—art, sport, and so on. The important thing is feeling one’s existence. Some may choose a kind of specialisation—barbering, sewing, and the like—because it makes them feel that others need them.

The reason is not hard to identify. After dinner I walked in the dorm. I did not want to do anything.

I noticed that my movement was nervous in a certain way. It is tension, buried tension that does not clearly show itself.

Often we are not fully aware of what is happening inside us.

Today it became certain that the president will come to parliament the day after tomorrow for the constitutional oath. Almost certainly he will give an important speech. We are waiting.

In the evening I looked from the window. Snow was still holding on in some places.

But in the places it had left days ago, the green grass had begun to lift its head.

The green was beginning to colour the earth and would soon cover it, just as the snow had done a few days earlier.

The smell of spring was coming to me through the window, through the soft breeze touching my face. I may as well try to sleep, if I can; it is the best solution now.

11-3-92

Damascus Radio announced that the president will give a speech before noon tomorrow, dealing with internal and external issues, and drawing the features of the next stage of the country’s life. Waiting is now more justified than before, and anticipation has become very natural.

We will wait to hear what the president says tomorrow, because we expect our fate to be decided through his words. Yes, we feel that tomorrow he will judge us.

Continuation of 11-3-92

Maybe even simple work, and even making small or trivial things like gifts, is important. Maybe it has a double role: it makes us work, produce, give, and feel our existence in relation to others.

It seems to me this is something natural in the human being, deep and perhaps instinctive.

Besides work, there is reading and trying to catch new things, and listening to the radio for new things. Sometimes the goal is not knowledge itself, but something else—maybe argument, or discussion. Maybe the hidden goal is proving one’s existence to the people here first.

Some people are satisfied with proving their existence here so they can feel balanced.

Reading and gathering information for discussion may be one of these methods. Excelling in something else may be another: art, sport, and so on. The important thing is feeling one’s existence. Some may choose a kind of specialisation—barbering, sewing, and the like—because it makes them feel that others need them.

I say that such behaviour may come from the need to create balance, though of course it can come from other motives too.

Sometimes a person fails to create this balance, even after many attempts, often without being aware of them. Then I think deep problems strike them, problems that lead to different psychological symptoms of different depth and intensity.

In any case, I have long thought about this issue and tried to build a theory around it, but mostly only inside myself. Very rarely has it been a subject of discussion with others.

I think the search for this balance is not limited to prison, but it appears more strongly here because it is harder to achieve. Outside prison there are many more ways to find it. Even belonging to a political organisation may be one of those ways. I will not enter that topic now. I simply say that the breakdown of this balance can lead to a degree of madness, to a disturbance in the nervous system.

One of our young men threw himself into theatre—preparing, directing, acting—with huge effort and very long hours, and he achieved excellent results. His inner energies were very large, and all of this gave them space. I watched him carefully. In the days before presenting a play, he became a completely different man, a man with no connection to prison.

At that moment he felt free. Yes, he felt that he was living moments of complete freedom. But after that, everything turned upside down again.
