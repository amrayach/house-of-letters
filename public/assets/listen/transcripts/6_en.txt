Paper 33: first side

Continuation: 5-3-92

The days are still passing slowly, and the hours are still dragging. The date is now close and is measured in hours, not days. This waiting is different from every other time.

The narcissus has fully opened, and its smell is strong and beautiful. Where are you now, Ola? You must be asleep. Are you dreaming of Baba, soon? Are you dreaming that he kisses you after a long wait, before you sleep? Are you waiting for Baba, worried that he may not come? Will you cry after that? My heart is with you, my dear little girl.

And you, my little one, who has never really known the taste of Baba’s goodnight kisses, or his bedtime stories, or even his songs—you do not know any of that at all.

Other children turn to their father, not to a grandfather or an uncle. My little one, I am waiting for such a day in a way you cannot imagine.

I hope that this time everyone’s dream comes true.

It is another day of waiting and worry. This waiting and this worry will reach their peak tomorrow. If the news we received is true, then a wave of releases will begin tomorrow, Saturday 7/3. Today was ordinary. I spent a long time during the day and evening working on Randa’s gift for the end of March. I worked for a long time despite the pain in my back, pain unlike anything I have felt for a long time. I had to get a massage in the afternoon and again in the evening. Just a little while ago Hassan rubbed the painful area after using a hot-water bag, and I feel some improvement now.

Paper 33: second side

Continuation: 6-3-92

From the window. The weather was beautiful. Most of the snow had melted, the earth had started to breathe, and the spring breeze was blowing.

Right away I thought: could I soon breathe this fresh air in another place? In a free place? Could I breathe it whenever I want, where I want? Spring is coming, and freedom would be wonderful with it.

8-3-92

It is International Women’s Day. Happy day to you, Randa. Happy day to you, Umm Ahd.

The list begins and will not end. Happy day to you, women—the partners in life, the partners in everything.

Two days have passed, the 7th and the 8th, and they were considered critical days when something might happen. Nothing happened, of course, neither bad nor good, and we heard no new news.

I changed the water in the vase of narcissus. I sighed a little and went back to reading.

Only a few days remain, and we will either be free or more miserable than before and more empty of hope. In that context, today I thought of writing something about madness in prison. News that someone has gone mad has become something almost normal.

This madness that some of the Brothers think is something special, even treating one case almost like a saint. In our wing there is Abd al-Karim, who confused me. At first I thought he was drunk. Later it became clear that he was ill. He had truly lost an important part of his control over himself.

Why do people go mad? Some reasons seem known or at least possible to identify. Sometimes there are direct events, and sometimes there are not. But the special general conditions here strongly help produce that result. I think this comes from what I call the loss of balance—an inner, psychological balance.

We all need that balance, or rather a lot of it. How? I think we need to love and be loved, to give and receive, to feel our own existence and the existence of others.

Here, first of all because of the lack of freedom, and sometimes because of even harsher conditions,

it is hard to find that balance.

When we lose everyone for whom we feel a deep real affection, how can we love and be loved? When we lose any real chance to work or to give, how can we give and receive? When we live alone or almost alone, how can we feel the presence of others, and know that we still exist for them?
