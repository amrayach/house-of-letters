Smiling at the thought of it, I stayed a long time with the memory of my first evening with Randa and my last evening with her, the day I danced with Ola, and the day she and Lana played freely, and how happy we were with them. After that, the evenings went from bad to worse.

When Anwar asked me where Randa could spend the evening, and whether I knew, I do not know what I felt when I answered that I did not know the place anymore after it had been taken and lived in. Was it defeat? Was it helplessness? Was it deprivation? I do not know, but it was a painful tightness and burning feeling.

Did Randa overcome her sadness that night? Did she overcome her fears and thoughts? Did her tears lose the battle?

12-1-90

Yesterday was Randa’s birthday, and it was also my visiting day. Randa came, with the children and the whole family.

In the evening the comrades held a small celebration.

For the first time, I spoke with my mother yesterday—maybe this had already been discussed with my father too—about leaving prison and leaving the party as a condition for that.

Randa encouraged me and stood by me. She said, ‘I am with you.’ Yes, Randa, and we will stay together.

What worries me is the tension I started to feel yesterday between Randa and the family, maybe most of them, as if they want her to pressure me, or as if they feel she is in some way responsible for what happened.

It pains me to see what Randa is carrying, on many levels and in every way.

Happy birthday to you. It would be beautiful if we are together next year.

17-1-90

With whom will you go back in the dark road? There is no one.

Randa said she did not spend New Year’s Eve with anyone. She stayed at home. Why, Randa?

The smell of narcissus is wonderful, especially when it comes from little Ola and from Randa.

On my small table, your flowers, Randa and Ola, are there in front of me.

The first thing I see when I open my eyes in the morning is the flowers, and the first thing I remember is both of you, and I cannot help but smile.

Today is Wednesday. Randa is listening to Fairuz now, and so am I. O moon, you and I. Beautiful moon, full moon, when you used to watch us on the slopes of Qasioun. Have you changed, or are you still the same moon? How could I forget you in our very few nights, in our new house in Masyaf?

‘He said: O house of ours, beside you the stars lived... night after night... until the road was completed... your house is far and the night does not let him return... we are the ones who deserve you most.’

A week ago I saw Randa’s eyes, especially when she spoke about New Year’s Eve.
