Night of 11/10/88 - Morning of 12/10/88

A year has passed since the last time I saw Masyaf and walked in its streets, and a year has passed since the last time I entered my home and sat with my family.

A year has passed since the last time I sat on a chair and breathed the air of Masyaf’s mountains. Winter is coming and has started to take over the weather. The cold has begun, the clouds are there, and maybe it has rained in Masyaf.

The days pass, and so do the weeks and months, and now I can add years. These security bars stand between us and life, and between us and people. Why? I live now, and everyone is with me in my memory.

The photo of Randa and the children near me pulls me toward them. They must be asleep now. My father, my mother, my brothers and sisters, my uncles on both sides, Randa’s family home, my uncle’s friends. I remember them all. I live with them in my mind. I love them.

A year has passed, and I do not know how much more will pass. I am still alive, and I will go on. Ola and Alaa will wait for me, hug me, and kiss me. Randa will wait for me too, and together we will make up for what was lost and build our future again.

19/12/88

Yesterday snow fell. It fell hard and heavily, covering the ground, the trees, and the houses. I could not see the nearby mountains because the snowfall was so thick.

This is what nature looked like today.

Can prison really imprison us on a day like this? Can these walls and bars stop the soul from jumping outside and moving through these beautiful fields and roads? No, they could not.

I remember, and I like the memory: the snow, Bloudan and Masyaf. What would the snow be like in Masyaf? I think the view from our house must be beautiful. I hope the children play in it. I hope they laugh and feel happy.

Bloudan: four adults, or rather six people, with two wonderful little children, in a red car with two bottles of red wine and a bag of salty snacks. A beautiful sunny day. The girls liked the taste of the wine, and it became hard to take the bottle away from them. They did not want the pistachios anymore. What little devils they were.

The snow is falling heavily. It is a real snowstorm, the road is still long, and the car is almost alone. My wife and I are there, and with us a small baby still in its mother’s womb. The snow thickens on the road and it becomes hard to drive fast.

At last the black asphalt appears, and you continue your trip. You look at your wife with love and keep going. Snow and sun—nothing shows freedom more strongly.

25-26/2/89

It is the twenty-fifth of February. Seven years have passed since that beautiful day, the day my full shared life with Randa began. Seven years that brought many changes in me and in Randa, and two wonderful children, Ola and Alaa. A year and a half of distance, pain, and deprivation has not moved me away from them even by an inch.

Today I go back to live with them in the minutes of life. I remember them, draw their faces, bring back their words and jokes, and try to laugh.

Life is full of contradiction. It should move toward something better. It should become safer, happier, and freer. My wife, my beloved, how close you are.

My beloved, my friend, my companion, my wife and partner—I am sorry. I did not want to be away from you and from my two children.

On this day I send you my longing and give you a white rose, a handful of jasmine, and a kiss. I come back to tell you, with all my hope, that our most beautiful days are the ones we have not lived yet, and that the most beautiful words are still the ones I have not said to you.

30/8/89

Today is Wednesday, and Fairuz is singing. It is the weekly time, the weekly meeting. Randa is listening to Fairuz now, I am sure of it.

Yes, dear Randa, this weekly meeting that brings us together through Fairuz is beautiful. It is as beautiful as Fairuz’s voice, and only our meeting together could be more beautiful.

After you, in my heart, there is the rose of roses, opening petal by petal. How wonderful you are, Fairuz. How wonderful you are, Randa. How close you are to my heart. You are the flower of life. You are poetry and love, and I add this myself: you are life.

How long will this separation last? Almost two years have passed since that miserable day, the day carved forever into my memory, and I think into yours too.

Do you see how big the sea is? That is how big my love for you is. Do you remember the sea, Randa? We often used to visit it around these days. Do you remember the sea, and the colour of your eyes, and those beautiful times?

My beloved, my sea, I miss you deeply. I long for you even more.

‘You steal me from sleep, from among all the others, and with the ice of the past you burn me.’ Bravo, Fairuz.

This will not last forever. Everything has an end. Even sad and bad days end, and even the lives of powerful people must come to an end.

31/12/89

These are the last hours, the last night of this year, and even of this decade. At this time tomorrow, we will write 1990. We will write 9 instead of 8, after ten years in which this number stood inside modern history.

But I remember some beautiful things clearly: the first New Year’s Eve I spent with Randa, on that winter day, in a house in Sahat al-Qusour. The evening began nicely, then her tooth pain became worse, her temperature rose, and after that we had to cut the evening short.

After that we spent evenings in Masyaf. I remember a photo of Georgette. I do not remember everything clearly, but I follow the years one after another: 83, 84, 85, 86. Then I become sad when I remember 87 and the sadness of that year, then 88 and 89, and now three years here. To be continued.

1/1/90

It is the first day of the last decade of this century: 1-1-90. Last night passed between joy and sadness, between one beautiful memory and another painful one. Maybe there will be three more nights like it. Even with some people’s optimism, and even with hope, I still think there are more numbers for us to count.
