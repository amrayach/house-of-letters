Continuation of 19-5-92

Many moments passed through my memory.

Perhaps the last of them was also connected to al-Qadmus, but with Randa—yes, with Randa, with my beloved who loved al-Qadmus.

How beautiful freedom is, and how much I long for it. How much I long for a breeze like that, cool and light, coming into my blanket and making me shiver, while I am free. And how good it would be if Randa were with me, because if she were with me I would not feel the cold.

These are our dreams, our support in this place.

20-5-92

I am tired of this waiting. More than two weeks have passed since the trial began and my turn still has not come. Yes, the boredom has become very great.

Even reading, my constant comfort, no longer attracts me.

When even my desire to read drops this low, it means the boredom inside me has reached a very high level. That is how things are these days.

It is clear that the pace of the trials has increased, and the number of statements being recorded each day has become larger than before. It also seems that visits have been cut off for a while, which means we are entering a dry period on top of everything else. The old saying fits these days: may God protect us from what is worse.

21-5-92

I do not know how I forgot yesterday. I meant to record that a full year had passed since the day I was asked to submit a request to Security and announce that I was leaving the party in order to be released.

I remembered only when I was trying to sleep, after turning off the light and lying down.

A year ago, on this same day, it never crossed my mind that I would still be here. I did not imagine I would spend another full year in detention. But the days, weeks, and months passed, and now a whole year has gone by—leap year included. I do not know how it passed.

A full year passed in which everyone here treated me as if I were only a temporary resident, a guest. Even the more cautious ones gave that up and began giving me advice for outside. Some even shared news with me because they thought I would be leaving them soon. But day after day it became clear that what was ahead of us was only a mirage.

Without doubt, it was the harshest period of my detention.

Continuation of 21-5-92

Measured in pain, anxiety, and waiting, this one year equals much more than the whole period before it. Before that, detention was hard but bearable, and we were still living in a somewhat reasonable way. After that, everything turned upside down.

That is how it has been for us here. So what about the family and loved ones outside? I think it was worse for them. Their suffering was heavier, their days longer than ours, and their year longer than ours. Their hope was bigger, and so their disappointment was more bitter.

I know well how much pain I placed on them and how much misery hit them. How hard it must have been to feel disappointment again and again. In practice, the loved ones outside were imprisoned like me, and sometimes even more than me. Or at least they suffered like me, and sometimes more.

After all that has happened, what do I dare to dream of? Honestly, nothing. I do not dare any dream now. I have become afraid of every beautiful dream, every new dream of freedom.

23-5-92

It was a very exhausting day, a kind of fatigue I rarely felt in detention—physical exhaustion, nervous strain, headache. It was the day of the trial, the first hearing, the session for recording statements, as they call it. My meeting with the judge lasted about twenty-five minutes.

If that session has any value, and if the whole trial is not just a formality,

and if the judge takes

my statements into account and is convinced by them, then the result could be good. In my statement I confirmed that I had cut my connection with the party in mid-1985 and that afterwards my relations were social only.

I want to write a lot about seeing Damascus again after about four years and eight months away. I want to write about that day’s trip. But my great tiredness forces me to postpone it.

27-5-92

For days I have wanted to return to writing, but may God forgive Abu Lana.

I wanted to record the story that caught me by the throat when we reached the capital, and the pain that ran in my blood, especially when we came level with al-Tijara and the house of Uncle Abu Nidal.

Here on this road, from Uncle Abu Nidal’s house to the court, Ain al-Karsh, this dear road—I could remember nothing except my walks with Randa, the walks that so often began at the university and passed through Ain al-Karsh to al-Tijara. Yes, that was what took over my thoughts most.
