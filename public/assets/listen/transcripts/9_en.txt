Continuation of 27-5-92

My memory brought back many images and many events, especially those that used to be tied to these places every day: Ola and her kindergarten near this road, and her teacher, or caretaker, who once cried when we moved Ola to another kindergarten.

Still, my walks with Randa in those days, when we were in love,

take up the biggest space in my memories.

6-6-92

The stagnation we are living through now has no example before it. It is swamp-like stagnation, a swamp that produces nothing but disease, bad smells, and harmful mosquitoes.

It is the first time we have lived through a state like this: visits have stopped for everyone.

That means we are cut off from news from the world close to us at the very moment when we are desperate for any news in this critical period. It also means material need. In practice we have almost nothing to eat except what the prison gives us. Many are hungry, especially when tea does not come, as in these days. No tea, no coffee, no mate—only water. A spoon of labneh, one potato, very little yogurt, and bulgur. That is a whole day’s food.

This swamp-like state is very unhealthy for our social life. It is the best ground for tension and friction, even if it stays verbal.

I have a feeling—not a vague feeling, but one based on thinking and analysis—that the result of the trial will be bad, maybe even disastrous. I expect harsh sentences unlike anything we have seen before.

And I think my sentence may be above the middle range. So another period of waiting lies ahead of me.

Second side

Continuation of 6-6-92

Another period, and not a short one, that I may spend in detention. I have perhaps started to train myself for that.

It has reached the point that I now wish this trial would end as fast as possible, that the verdicts would be issued and known, and that we would get the chance to complete the detention period in a civil prison. I even thought about that and decided I would choose Hama prison if there were any choice. Even so, nothing can settle now, and all that stands before me is the worst kind of waiting.

8-6-92

It was like a flash, like a dream. After nearly five years, I knew him the moment my eyes met his, even from far away.

I saw some faces I did not recognise. I did not recognise Abu Lana either until I looked into his eyes.

I felt happiness, real happiness.

To see a friend after all this time.

He had not changed much.

The brightness in his eyes,

the moustache that had grown, and that gentle smile.

This was really the first time I had seen him, and that made me long even more for the meeting. Will it really happen?

11-6-92

The first day of Eid is over. Yes, it is the tenth Eid I have spent imprisoned.

I still have one dream, a real dream: to spend an Eid day with Ola and Alaa now that they are old enough for the special joy of Eid. I want to see how they wake up on Eid morning, how they care about their new clothes,

and how eager they are to go out and play.

What a beautiful dream it is to wake up on Eid morning to the sound of the takbir and have a special breakfast with Randa and the children.

An Eid day. How much I miss such a day—a free Eid day. Ten Eids is not a small number.

Eid is another day. It does not bring anything new,

but I cannot deal with it like any other day.

It means something special to me. It makes me feel things different from the rest of the days.

Another Eid, another sadness—an even bigger sadness.
