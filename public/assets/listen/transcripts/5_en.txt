15-1-92

This is the first time I really notice that the year is 92 and not 91, except on New Year’s Eve.

This day has a special taste. A few hours ago, fourteen years passed since the first time I met Randa and spoke to her. The fifteenth year has now begun. It is the fifth time this occasion has come while we are apart, and of course it is the most painful.

Because I had prepared a special celebration for it, at least in my mind, just as I had done for Randa’s birthday.

The news coming in says that there is no harm in dreaming such a dream.

Even so, I woke up dreaming. I dreamed of my release. I saw myself arriving and meeting some loved ones. Nuha stepped back in shock, Nasser rushed toward me kissing me, and Randa—I could not even understand what she was doing because at that same moment Abu al-Ahd appeared, held his chest, and had an attack. Then I was running around looking for a doctor, until I woke up upset because I had not found one and I had not seen how the dream ended.

Yes, it is the waking dream that scares me—the fear that Umm al-Ahd or Abu al-Ahd might face some crisis either when I am released or before. That is truly what scares me very much.

My imagination also took me back to that evening fourteen years ago.

I lived again in the hall of the Faculty of Engineering. I waited, smiled, remembered, and laughed.

This is the fifth time here that I have replayed this tape of memories.

In fact, maybe it is the thousandth time I have lived those moments, but they become more intense on this day.

I also wondered once again about Rania and how she was doing, and remembered that for a long time I have known nothing about her news with Jamal.

A month has now passed since the release of the prisoners started. Even though a month has passed, I cannot say it was short. It definitely was not.

Continuation of 15-1-92

Randa, my constant companion, my companion on the road for almost fourteen years—how are you now? How are you, my beloved? Honestly, I feel a kind of worry for you, especially for your health.

Why does the radio do this to me? Nagat comes to sing, ‘The eyes of the heart are awake,’ and I find myself changing the words, asking sleep to leave the eyes of my beloved so he can dream this day of that day from fourteen years ago and remember it.

‘My beloved, ah my beloved... he has the sweetest smile. When his eyes smile, I say, God keep him safe.’ You know, Randa, how precious your smile is to me and how happy I am when I see it. And as for your eyes, I do not need to say anything more—what if they smile too?

Yes, Randa, I miss you. I miss you very much. My soul has dried up, and there is no water for it except with you, in our home, inside freedom. If I did not see you once every month, it would die.

16-1-92

Another day has passed, a day I wished and dreamed would not come while I was still here. It is another day of a different kind as well.

It has now been four years, four months, and two days since my arrest. What is special about that?

What is special is that this amount of time matches the age of my dear little Ola on the day I was arrested. She was four years, four months, and two days old then. Today she must be eight years, eight months, and four days old. Her age has doubled while I have been in prison away from her. In other words, starting tomorrow, the days I have lived with Ola while imprisoned will be more than the days I lived with her while free. It is a new fact, and a sad one.

So this number, this date—I calculated it long ago and waited for it, hoping it would never come. But like all my hopes connected to release, it was blown away.
